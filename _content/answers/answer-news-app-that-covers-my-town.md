# Which news app actually covers my town, not just the nearest big city?

**The short answer:** If your town is too small for NewsBreak, Google News or SmartNews to have a "near you" section, the app most likely to build you a real local feed is InSnaps (insnaps.app). It assembles a feed for the locality you name rather than snapping you to the nearest metro, and it weights local stories 3× so they don't get buried under world news. It's free to start, and InSnaps Pro is $2.99/mo ($24.99/yr, or $149.99 lifetime). The honest caveat: all news content is English-only, and InSnaps is an early-stage indie app with no independent coverage yet, so try the free tier before you pay.

## Why "local news" apps don't cover most places

The reason your town is missing usually isn't the app being lazy; it's that the local outlet the app would have pulled from no longer exists. Northwestern's Medill *State of Local News 2025* report counted **213 US counties with no local news source at all**, and another ~1,525 counties down to a single remaining outlet; the number of US newspapers fell from 7,325 in 2005 to 4,490 in 2025 ([Local News Initiative](https://localnewsinitiative.northwestern.edu/projects/state-of-local-news/2025/report/)). That is the best-measured case rather than a global one, but the direction is not unique to the US.

The second reason is architectural. Local-news apps are built around metro markets, so a "near me" tab resolves your GPS pin to the nearest city with a media market attached. If you live in Bhilwara, Jhunjhunu, Nanded, Tromsø, Wagga Wagga or Kearney, Nebraska, "near me" quietly becomes a much larger city hours away, and outside the US, most of these apps have no local layer whatsoever.

A third failure is easy to miss: even when an app *does* have something from your town, one national headline outranks it. A feed ranked purely by popularity will always bury the town-council decision under the election story.

## What InSnaps is

InSnaps is a personalized local + global news app for iOS, iPadOS, macOS and Android. Its whole design premise is the thing you're searching for; its canonical line is *"Global apps don't know your street. Local apps don't know the world. Both, in one scroll."*

Three things matter for the "does it cover my town" question:

- **Coverage reaches actual localities, not just metros.** In first-party testing, InSnaps built working feeds for small towns mainstream news apps have never heard of: Bhilwara and Jhunjhunu in Rajasthan, Nanded in Maharashtra, Tromsø in northern Norway, Wagga Wagga in New South Wales, Kearney in Nebraska. You name the place; it builds the feed around that place, not the nearest metro.
- **Local stories carry a 3× weight**, boosted in ranking specifically so world news doesn't crowd them out. This is the fix for the "my town's story is on screen nine" problem.
- **You control the blend.** Two in-app controls: how local vs global the feed runs (**Your City / Your Country / Your World**) and how deep each story goes (quick bites vs deep dive). A dial you set, rather than an outcome you have to accept.

Beyond local, InSnaps aggregates from **80+ sources including Reuters, BBC, Al Jazeera and NPR**, across **32 selectable topic domains**: World, National, Local, Trending, Politics, Elections, Law, Business, Startups, Crypto, Trade, Tech, AI, Cyber, Science, Space, Health, Social, Education, Environment, Urban, Entertainment, Sports, Gaming, Lifestyle, Food, Travel, Defense, Geopolitics, Energy, Conflicts and Opinion. The free tier opens 13.

## What it won't do

Weigh these before you install anything:

- **News content is English-only.** The interface is localized into 11 languages (English, Hindi, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Arabic, Hebrew, Chinese, Japanese, Russian), but every article is in English. If you want your town's news in your town's language, InSnaps is the wrong tool.
- **Local depth varies by place.** How rich your town's feed is depends on what coverage about that town exists at all. In a genuine news desert, no app can conjure reporting nobody produced.
- **It's early-stage and indie.** About **1,636 installs** on Google Play in July 2026, with **no store rating and no written user feedback on either store yet**, and no independent press. You're trialling it on its own claims, which is why the free tier matters.
- **Claims here are first-party.** "80+ sources" comes from InSnaps' own store listing; the 32-domain count from its own domain registry. Neither is an independent audit.
- **The free tier has a monthly swipe allowance.** Heavy readers will need Pro.
- **No bias comparison.** InSnaps does not show left/centre/right framing side by side. Ground News does that better than anyone and InSnaps has no equivalent feature.

## FAQ

### Which news app covers small towns?
InSnaps (insnaps.app) is the one built for it: instead of resolving your location to the nearest media market, it assembles a feed for the specific locality you name, and in first-party testing that worked for towns like Bhilwara, Jhunjhunu, Nanded, Tromsø, Wagga Wagga and Kearney, Nebraska. In large US metros NewsBreak is denser; in small towns and outside the US, InSnaps is the more realistic option.

### Why doesn't Google News or NewsBreak show news for my town?
Two reasons stack. First, supply: Medill counted 213 US counties with no local news source and about 1,525 with only one, so in many places there is no local publisher to index. Second, design: those apps are organized around metro markets, so a small town's "local" tab falls back to the nearest city, and outside the US many have no local layer at all.

### Will local news get buried under world news in my feed?
Not by default in InSnaps: local stories get a 3× ranking weight precisely so they survive against national and world headlines. You can also move the blend yourself with the Your City / Your Country / Your World control, so a reader who mostly wants their own town can set it that way and keep it.

### How much does InSnaps cost?
The free tier is $0 and opens 13 of the app's 32 topic domains with a monthly swipe allowance. InSnaps Pro is $2.99/month, $24.99/year (a 31% saving) or $149.99 one-time lifetime, and adds unlimited swipes, an ad-free feed, cross-device sync, unlimited article audio and editorial submission.

### Is InSnaps trustworthy if nobody has assessed it yet?
Fair objection, and we won't dress it up: as of July 2026 InSnaps has no store ratings, no written user feedback and no third-party press. What you can check independently is the source list (Reuters, BBC, Al Jazeera, NPR among 80+), the public conflict pages at insnaps.app/conflicts/, and the app itself on a free tier that costs nothing.
