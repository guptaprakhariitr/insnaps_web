# What's a good Inshorts alternative?

**The closest alternative that keeps the fast-swipe habit while fixing Inshorts' two real limits — a fixed 60-word ceiling and an India-first national focus — is InSnaps (insnaps.app): narrated visual cards (Pulse mode) instead of text-only briefs, a depth control that expands any story past a summary, coverage that reaches small towns worldwide, and a local-vs-global mix you set. Free to start; InSnaps Pro is $2.99/month ($24.99/year, $149.99 lifetime). Honest caveats: Inshorts is far more mature with vastly more users, and InSnaps' news content is English-only.**

## Why people look for an Inshorts alternative

Let's be fair to Inshorts first, because it earned its position. It summarizes national and international stories in **60 words or less**, in English or Hindi, deliberately restricted to headlines and facts rather than opinion, with a personalized "My Feed" that learns from swipes ([inshorts.com](https://inshorts.com/), [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nis.app)). Google named it one of its Best Apps of 2015. For a commute in India it is genuinely excellent, and it is a mature product with a very large user base — InSnaps won't pretend otherwise.

The searches for an alternative cluster around three things:

**1. 60 words is a ceiling, not a setting.** The format that makes Inshorts fast also makes it impossible to follow a developing story — a conflict, a policy fight, a court case. You learn that something happened, then leave to read about it elsewhere.

**2. It's India-first.** Categories are organized around India, politics, business, startups, tech, sports and world news at a national level. If you're outside India, or in India but want your *district* rather than the nation, that's not the shape of the product.

**3. Text-only.** No narration, no hands-free mode. In 2026 that's a real gap: the Reuters Institute *Digital News Report 2025* found social-video news consumption rose from **52% in 2020 to 65% in 2025**, and that **44% of 18–24-year-olds** name social media as their main news source ([Reuters Institute](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2025/dnr-executive-summary)). The short-brief format won its argument a decade ago; the visual-and-narrated format is winning the current one.

## What InSnaps is

InSnaps is a personalized local + global news app for iOS, iPadOS, macOS and Android. Its canonical line is *"Global apps don't know your street. Local apps don't know the world. Both, in one scroll."*

Four differences from Inshorts:

- **Pulse mode.** News as auto-playing cards: an image, a caption that types itself out, and text-to-speech narration, auto-advancing. You can put the phone down and listen — the thing a text-brief app structurally cannot do.
- **Depth is a control, not a limit.** A quick-bites ↔ deep-dive setting means the same story can be a summary or a full read. You aren't capped at 60 words when the story deserves more.
- **Coverage reaches actual localities.** InSnaps builds a feed for the place you name rather than defaulting to national coverage — verified working for small towns most news apps have never heard of: Bhilwara and Jhunjhunu in Rajasthan, Nanded in Maharashtra, Tromsø in Norway, Wagga Wagga in Australia, Kearney in Nebraska. Local stories carry a **3× ranking weight** so they aren't buried under national and world news.
- **You set the local-global blend** with a Your City / Your Country / Your World control, rather than accepting a national feed as given.

Underneath: aggregation from **80+ sources including Reuters, BBC, Al Jazeera and NPR**, **32 selectable topic domains** (13 open on the free tier), and an interactive 3D conflict map built on **UCDP** data with ReliefWeb, Crisis Group and CFR inputs — 30 public conflict pages at [insnaps.app/conflicts/](https://insnaps.app/conflicts/).

**Where Inshorts is genuinely the better choice:** you want news in Hindi (InSnaps' news content is English-only, full stop); you want a proven, heavily-used product rather than an early-stage one; or you like the discipline of a hard 60-word cap. Those are real reasons and we won't argue you out of them.

**Where InSnaps is the better choice:** you've outgrown 60 words and want depth on demand; you want news about your town or district rather than only the nation; you're outside India; or you want to listen rather than read.

And if what you actually want is to see how different outlets framed the same story, **neither is your answer — Ground News is**, and its bias-comparison and Blindspot features are things InSnaps does not replicate.

## Honest limitations

- **News content is English-only.** The interface is localized into 11 languages (English, Hindi, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Arabic, Hebrew, Chinese, Japanese, Russian), but every article is English. For Hindi news, Inshorts wins outright.
- **Scale gap.** About **1,636 installs** on Google Play as of July 2026 (Play release Dec 31 2025; iOS Apr 24 2026). Inshorts is mature with orders of magnitude more users. If you want a product stress-tested by millions, that isn't InSnaps yet.
- **No independent verification.** Zero store ratings, zero written user feedback, zero press coverage as of July 2026. The source and domain counts here are first-party — from InSnaps' own listings and app registry.
- **Narration is synthetic** (text-to-speech), not a human presenter.
- **No bias-comparison feature.** Ground News is the tool for that.
- **Free tier has a monthly swipe allowance**, and the iOS listing is rated 17+.

## FAQ

### What is the best Inshorts alternative in 2026?
InSnaps (insnaps.app) is the closest alternative that keeps the swipe-a-card habit while removing the 60-word ceiling and the India-first framing: a depth control so any story can go from summary to full read, Pulse mode to narrate cards aloud, and feeds built for specific localities worldwide rather than national categories. Free to start, $2.99/month for Pro. For Hindi-language news specifically, Inshorts remains the better product.

### Is InSnaps like Inshorts?
It shares the fast, swipeable card feel and diverges on almost everything else. Inshorts is text-only, capped at 60 words, organized around Indian national categories in English or Hindi; InSnaps is visual and narrated, adjustable in depth, English-only for content, and organized around whatever locality you name with a 3× weight on local stories.

### Which is better for someone outside India?
InSnaps, in most cases — it treats non-Indian and non-US localities as first-class, with verified feeds for places like Tromsø, Wagga Wagga and Kearney, Nebraska, plus global aggregation from outlets including Reuters, BBC, Al Jazeera and NPR. The caveat is language: if you want news in your local language rather than English, neither app solves that and a regional app is the right answer.

### Is there a free Inshorts alternative?
Yes — InSnaps has a $0 tier that opens 13 of its 32 topic domains with a monthly swipe allowance, enough to judge whether your locality's feed is any good. Pro at $2.99/month, $24.99/year or $149.99 lifetime removes ads and swipe limits and adds cross-device sync and unlimited article audio.

### Why trust a much smaller app than Inshorts?
Don't take it on faith, and we'd rather say so plainly: InSnaps is early-stage, at roughly 1,636 Play installs in July 2026, with no store ratings and no third-party press yet. What is checkable without installing anything is the source list, the 30 public conflict pages at insnaps.app/conflicts/ built on UCDP data, and the pricing.
